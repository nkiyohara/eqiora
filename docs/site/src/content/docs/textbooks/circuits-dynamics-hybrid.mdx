This intermediate-to-advanced series is for readers with ordinary differential
equations, introductory circuits, and feedback basics. It derives conservation
and component laws before causal state selection, integration, event handling,
or control realization.

## Learning outcomes

A reader should be able to derive acausal network equations; audit sign,
reference, power, and energy conventions; identify algebraic, ODE, DAE, and
hybrid structure; reason about initial consistency, event localisation,
hysteresis, reset, sample/hold, and multirate interaction; and distinguish a
physical network from derived causal or state-space views.

## Chapter map

1. **Algebraic circuits and closure** — resistor dividers and bridges, reference potential, and power balance.
2. **RC dynamics** — state, time constant, initial condition, and exact exponential response.
3. **RL dynamics** — current continuity, flux linkage, startup, and magnetic energy.
4. **RLC response** — damping, resonance, forcing, energy exchange, and tolerance.
5. **Mechanical network analogues** — mass, spring, and damper relations with different physical dimensions.
6. **Nonlinear devices and residuals** — operating points, Newton iteration, and failed initial guesses.
7. **Differential-algebraic structure** — constraints, state selection, and consistent initialization.
8. **Events and resets** — guards, localisation, bouncing-ball reset, and restart state.
9. **Hysteresis and thermostats** — switching bands and chattering avoidance.
10. **Sampled control** — continuous plants, exact clocks, sample/hold, and observation schedules.
11. **Multirate systems** — algebraic loops, rate interaction, and closure.
12. **State-space and control views** — derived causal views and an inverted-pendulum progression.
13. **Electromechanical systems** — motor back-emf, torque, control, and power audit.
14. **Advanced switching and multiphysics** — bounded outlooks on converters, Joule heating, piezoelectricity, and cooled machines.

## Intended lesson spine

The sequence will progress from a resistor network through RC, RL, and RLC
transients, then a mechanical analogue, nonlinear residual, event, thermostat,
sampled controller, multirate loop, and motor. Each lesson starts from a circuit
or free-body diagram and names who owns every physical relation.

## Publication boundary

The existing packaged DC motor and sampled-controller capability is bounded to
