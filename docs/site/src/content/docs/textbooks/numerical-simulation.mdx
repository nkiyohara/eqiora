This intermediate series is for readers who know calculus, linear algebra, and
the modeling foundations. It makes the path from continuous mathematics to a
trustworthy bounded computation visible without turning a smooth picture into
a numerical claim.

## Learning outcomes

A reader should be able to explain consistency, stability, convergence,
conditioning, conservation, nonlinear iteration, time-integration error, and
reproducibility; choose a bounded realization for a stated problem; isolate an
error source in a refinement study; and distinguish visual plausibility from a
credible numerical result.

## Chapter map

1. **From exact mathematics to finite computation** — approximation, floating point, truncation error, and iteration error.
2. **Linear algebra for simulation** — sparse systems, norms, conditioning, residual, and solution error.
3. **Root finding and nonlinear systems** — Newton linearization, damping, stopping criteria, and failure.
4. **Time integration** — explicit and implicit Euler, stability, stiffness, adaptive steps, and accepted states.
5. **Spatial grids and meshes** — Cartesian and simplex topology, geometry, correspondence, resolution, and quality.
6. **Finite differences as a conceptual bridge** — stencils and consistency without a textbook-only solver path.
7. **Finite element method** — trial and test spaces, weak forms, Q1/P1, assembly, and boundaries.
8. **Finite volume method** — control volumes, face fluxes, conservation, and cell-centred transport.
9. **Error norms and convergence** — L2, energy, and maximum norms; observed order and asymptotic range.
10. **Coupled space/time refinement** — holding one error small while measuring another.
11. **Verification and falsification** — closed forms, manufactured solutions, identities, and benchmark boundaries.
12. **Performance without self-deception** — work, memory, backends, reproducibility, and environment-local timing.

## Intended experiment spine

The sequence will use scalar and stiff decay, known-solution Poisson problems,
the same mathematical model through bounded FEM and FVM realizations, spatial
refinement, advection–diffusion, a nonlinear residual, and a conservation
mutant. Every study must say what remained fixed: Model, Geometry, mesh family,
time interval and step, tolerances, precision, backend, and observation norm.

## Publication boundary

Planned teaching failures include tolerance plateaus, unstable time stepping,
refinement outside the asymptotic range, singularity-driven order loss, poor
mesh quality, and nonconservative flux. Timings remain environment-specific.
The series is not an encyclopaedic numerical-analysis text, universal method
selector, benchmark suite, or source of new accepted values.
