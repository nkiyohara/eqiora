This intermediate series is for readers with thermodynamics, calculus, and
conservation laws. It develops energy and species balances, constitutive flux,
boundaries, time scales, and dimensionless interpretation before numerical
realization.

## Learning outcomes

A reader should be able to derive control-volume and local balances; distinguish
storage, diffusion, advection, source, reaction, and interface terms; choose and
audit Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, and continuity conditions; reason with Fourier,
Biot, Peclet, and related scales; and separate field error from global balance
error.

## Chapter map

1. **Energy and mass balances** — systems, control volumes, storage, flux, and source.
2. **Conduction and diffusion** — Fourier/Fick constitutive laws, dimensions, and signs.
3. **Steady one-dimensional slabs** — Dirichlet, Neumann, Robin, resistance, and flux.
4. **Composite media and interfaces** — temperature/concentration continuity and equal normal flux.
5. **Internal generation and reaction** — source balance and curved profiles.
6. **Transient diffusion** — characteristic time, initial conditions, and smooth decay.
7. **Multidimensional realization and refinement** — meshes, norms, and separated errors.
8. **Advection–diffusion** — Peclet number, numerical diffusion, conservation, and boundedness.
9. **Extended surfaces and distributed loss** — fin equations and analytic comparison when supported.
10. **Anisotropic transport** — tensor constitutive laws and directional response.
11. **Nonlinear and phase-change outlooks** — radiation and Stefan problems after shared capabilities exist.
12. **Coupled transport outlooks** — thermoelasticity, Joule heating, and conjugate heat transfer through shared multiphysics paths.

## Intended lesson spine

The planned progression starts with a steady slab, composite wall, internal
generation, and a smooth transient. Later lessons add multidimensional
refinement and conservative advection–diffusion. Field and integrated-flux
observations must be compared separately.

## Publication boundary

No textbook page may create a fin-, slab-, or phase-change-specific runner.
Anisotropy, radiation, phase change, and multiphysics remain outlooks until a
general product owner lands. Existing passive-scalar numerical claims do not
become a thermal component or material-library claim through documentation.
