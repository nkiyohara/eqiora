## A field needs somewhere to live

A scalar such as pressure assigns one value to each point of a region. A
velocity field assigns a spatial vector. Writing only a quantity and its unit
is therefore incomplete: a spatial model must also say which support carries
the field and what shape its values have.

Eqiora separates the semantic support from concrete geometry. A volume says
that a field lives in a region; its boundaries identify the places where the
interior model meets its surroundings. Meshes and discretization choices come
later.

## Learning outcomes

After this chapter, you should be able to:

- distinguish a field's support, representation, dimension, and value shape;
- read `grad`, `div`, `trace`, and `normal` by their mathematical roles;
- identify which quantities live in a volume and which are compared on its
  boundary; and
- diagnose an operator applied to the wrong kind of field.

## Name the region and its boundaries

The steady cylinder example declares one two-dimensional fluid volume and four
named parts of its boundary:

```eqiora
public support fluid: volume(ambient_dimension = 2);
public support inlet: boundary(parent = fluid);
public support outlet: boundary(parent = fluid);
public support walls: boundary(parent = fluid);
public support cylinder: boundary(parent = fluid);

representation space = continuum;
field velocity on fluid as space: m / s shape spatial_vector;
field pressure on fluid as space: kg / (m * s ^ 2) = 0;
```

`velocity` and `pressure` share the support `fluid`, but their value shapes
differ. The four boundary supports do not create four new fluids; they name
where distinct boundary relations apply.

## Read spatial operators by where they act

For a scalar field $p$ and vector field $\boldsymbol{u}$:

| Expression | Meaning | Where the result is used |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\nabla p$ | spatial rate of change of a scalar | in the volume |
| $\nabla\!\cdot\boldsymbol{u}$ | net outward tendency per unit volume | in the volume |
| $\operatorname{tr}\boldsymbol{u}$ | interior value restricted to a boundary | on the boundary |
| $\boldsymbol{T}\boldsymbol{n}$ | normal action of a tensor | on the boundary |

The operators also transform dimensions. If velocity has dimension $L/T$,
its divergence has dimension $1/T$. If pressure has dimension force per area,
its gradient has dimension force per volume. Shape and dimension are separate
checks, and both must make sense.

The model uses these roles directly:

```eqiora
relation incompressibility continuous on fluid {
  div(velocity) = 0;
}

relation wall_velocity continuous on walls {
  trace(velocity) = 0;
}
```

The first relation is an interior statement. The second compares the boundary
trace of the interior velocity with a prescribed zero velocity.

## Deliberate failure: divergence of a scalar

Replace the incompressibility relation with `div(pressure) = 0`. Pressure is a
scalar, while divergence here expects a spatial vector or tensor. The field is
still on the right support and its unit is still valid, but the operator has no
matching shape. Repair the mathematical statement; changing the mesh or solver
cannot make the expression meaningful.

## Exercises

1. State the support, dimension, and value shape of `velocity` and `pressure`.
2. Explain why `trace(velocity)` belongs in a relation on `walls`, not in the
   volume relation for incompressibility.
3. If temperature has dimension $\Theta$, what dimension does its gradient
   have?
4. Add a named `symmetry` boundary to the support declarations. What physical
   relation would still need to be supplied before the name has meaning?
