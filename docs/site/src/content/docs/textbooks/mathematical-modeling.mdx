This foundations series is for science and engineering students who know
introductory calculus and linear algebra but may not yet distinguish a physical
model from its numerical realization. It teaches how phenomena become typed
relations before a discretization or solver is chosen.

<Aside type="note" title="Publication status">
  **In progress · 4 published foundation chapters · 1 executable simulation
  chapter · current <ReleaseIdentity />.** Chapters 1–3 establish model
  structure and compile-time checks. Chapter 4 runs one bounded scalar ODE and
  compares three observations with its closed form.
</Aside>

## Prerequisites

- algebra, introductory calculus, and linear algebra;
- basic programming literacy; and
- willingness to state assumptions, units, and missing relations explicitly.

## Learning outcomes

A reader should be able to identify systems, fields, unknowns, parameters,
supports, ports, domains, and observables; state conservation, constitutive,
compatibility, initial, boundary, interface, and event relations; check
dimensions; distinguish mathematical meaning from realization policy; and
diagnose underdetermined, overconstrained, inconsistent, or physically
meaningless models before simulation.

## Chapter map

1. [**Models are not simulations**](/textbooks/mathematical-modeling/models-not-simulations/) — phenomenon, assumptions, mathematical meaning, realization, execution, and observation. **Published · Illustrative.**
2. [**Quantities, dimensions, and units**](/textbooks/mathematical-modeling/quantities-dimensions-units/) — quantities, dimensions, consistency, and the boundary of dimensional checking. **Published · Illustrative.**
3. [**Algebraic relations and networks**](/textbooks/mathematical-modeling/algebraic-relations-networks/) — unknown counts, constraints, conservation, and acausal composition. **Published · Illustrative.**
4. [**Ordinary differential equations**](/textbooks/mathematical-modeling/ordinary-differential-equations/) — state, initial conditions, scalar decay, installed execution, and closed-form comparison. **Published · Checked · executable.**
5. **Fields and spatial domains** — scalar and vector fields, support, gradients, divergence, flux, and integral balance.
6. **Conservation laws** — storage, flux, source, and local versus integral statements.
7. **Constitutive laws** — empirical and fundamental relations across transport, mechanics, and networks.
8. **Boundary and interface conditions** — essential and natural meaning, continuity, and action/reaction.
9. **Scaling and nondimensionalization** — characteristic scales and dimensionless groups without embedding solver choices.
10. **Hierarchy and reusable components** — Components, parameters, bindings, occurrences, and model closure.
11. **Events and hybrid meaning** — guards, resets, sampled behavior, and continuous behavior.
12. **From Model to Result** — compile, inspect, resolve, run, observe, and interpret the result.

## Intended lesson spine

Small, hand-checkable problems will include a resistor network, RC decay,
mass–spring oscillation, one-dimensional diffusion balance, hydrostatic
pressure, Couette flow, axial-bar equilibrium, and a sampled controller. Each
lesson will include a deliberate dimensional, relation, boundary, or sign error
so diagnosis is taught alongside the successful path.

## Publication boundary

Most lessons are intended to be **Illustrative** or **Checked** through
dimensional identities and closed-form relations. None is executable until its
derivation, installed-release source, comparison, failure mode, exercises,
accessibility, and explicit limitations ship together. This series is not an
API manual, symbolic theorem prover, or promise of general equation coverage.
