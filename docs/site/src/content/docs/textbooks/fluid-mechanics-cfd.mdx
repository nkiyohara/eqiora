This intermediate-to-advanced series is for engineering readers with calculus,
vector calculus, conservation laws, and introductory numerical methods. It
derives fluid mechanics before choosing a numerical realization.

## Learning outcomes

A reader should be able to derive mass and momentum conservation; interpret
pressure, viscous stress, convection, diffusion, and storage; reason with
Reynolds and related dimensionless numbers; state meaningful boundaries;
distinguish Stokes from Navier–Stokes assumptions; and judge results through
balances, refinement, and observables rather than appearance.

## Chapter map

1. **Fluids, continuum assumptions, and scales** — density, viscosity, stress, and applicability.
2. **Fluid statics** — hydrostatic pressure and force with a closed-form comparison.
3. **Kinematics and conservation of mass** — material derivative, divergence, and incompressibility.
4. **Momentum and constitutive stress** — Cauchy momentum and Newtonian viscosity.
5. **Exact parallel flows** — Couette and Poiseuille velocity, shear, flux, and pressure drop.
6. **Dimensionless equations** — Reynolds, Strouhal, and Peclet numbers and dominant balance.
7. **Stokes flow** — low-inertia assumptions, boundaries, pressure gauge, and a bounded cylinder illustration.
8. **Convection and Navier–Stokes** — nonlinear transport and alternative mathematical forms.
9. **Numerical incompressibility** — mixed FEM, cell-centred FVM, pressure modes, and coupling.
10. **Transient startup flows** — initial conditions, accepted states, time-step effects, and transport scales.
11. **Flow around a cylinder** — geometry, boundaries, force conventions, startup, and developed flow.
12. **Kármán vortex shedding** — instability, Reynolds regime, duration, perturbation, phase, and Strouhal number.
13. **Verification of CFD results** — mass and force balance, analytic precursors, and space/time refinement.
14. **Beyond the introductory model** — bounded outlooks on turbulence, compressibility, multiphase flow, and moving boundaries.

## Intended executable spine

The progression will move from hydrostatics and exact Couette/Poiseuille
profiles through smooth velocity diffusion, scalar advection–diffusion, Stokes
flow, transient incompressible startup, and eventually a sufficiently resolved
cylinder wake. Physical simulation time must remain distinct from frame or
output cadence.
