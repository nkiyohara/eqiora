Turn a physical question into a mathematical model before choosing a numerical
method. This path assumes algebra, introductory calculus and basic programming.
Read in order, or choose the lesson closest to your question.

## Build a model

1. [Models are not simulations](/learn/mathematical-modeling/models-not-simulations/) — separate the phenomenon, assumptions, mathematical meaning, realization and observation.
2. [Quantities, dimensions, and units](/learn/mathematical-modeling/quantities-dimensions-units/) — reject dimensionally inconsistent relations and recognize what unit checking cannot prove.
3. [Algebraic relations and networks](/learn/mathematical-modeling/algebraic-relations-networks/) — count unknowns, constraints and conservation relations in an acausal network.
4. [Ordinary differential equations](/learn/mathematical-modeling/ordinary-differential-equations/) — derive scalar decay, run the installed example and compare with the closed-form solution.

## Extend it through space

5. [Fields and spatial domains](/learn/mathematical-modeling/fields-spatial-domains/) — place scalar and vector quantities on supports and interpret their spatial operators.
6. [Conservation laws](/learn/mathematical-modeling/conservation-laws/) — connect local and integral statements of storage, flux and source.
7. [Constitutive laws](/learn/mathematical-modeling/constitutive-laws/) — close a balance with material relations from transport and mechanics.
8. [Boundary and interface conditions](/learn/mathematical-modeling/boundary-interface-conditions/) — distinguish prescribed values, natural conditions, continuity and action–reaction.

## Put it to use

The ODE lesson includes an executable experiment using the named installed
release. The other lessons develop equations through derivations, compilation
examples, deliberate failures, and exercises.
